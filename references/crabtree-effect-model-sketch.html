Crabtree Effect Model Sketch

A compact model couples mDEB reserve dynamics to an Ohm's-law pathway splitter: glucose drives the Crabtree overflow, ethanol is produced by fermentation, and ethanol can later become a second respiratory substrate when glucose repression relaxes.

State And Flux Diagram

Minimal Equations

mDEB reserve backbone

\( \frac{dx_C}{dt}=j_{A,G}(G)-j_{E,C}-\mu x_C \)

\( j_{E,C}=\frac{v_{E,C}x_C}{K_{E,C}+x_C} \)

\( \frac{dV}{dt}=\mu V \)

External glucose, ethanol, and oxygen

\( j_{A,G}(G)=j_{G,\max}\frac{G}{K_G+G} \)

\( I_G(G)=\frac{1}{1+(G/K_{G,E})^n} \)

\( j_{A,\mathrm{EtOH}}=j_{\mathrm{EtOH},\max}\frac{\mathrm{EtOH}}{K_{\mathrm{EtOH}}+\mathrm{EtOH}}\frac{O_2}{K_{O,\mathrm{EtOH}}+O_2}I_G(G) \)

\( \frac{dG}{dt}=D(G_{in}-G)-\frac{j_{A,G}(G)V}{Y_{X,G}} \)

\( \frac{d\mathrm{EtOH}}{dt}=D(\mathrm{EtOH}_{in}-\mathrm{EtOH})+y_{\mathrm{EtOH}}j_FV-\frac{j_{A,\mathrm{EtOH}}V}{Y_{X,\mathrm{EtOH}}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{EtOH}}\mathrm{EtOH} \)

\( f_{O_2}(O_2)=k_O\frac{O_2}{K_O+O_2} \)

\( \frac{dO_2}{dt}=D(O_{2,in}-O_2)+k_La(O_{2,sat}-O_2)-q_{O_2}(j_R^G+j_R^{\mathrm{EtOH}})V \)

For a batch culture set \(D=0\). The default uses glucose repression rather than symmetric competition: ethanol is produced while glucose is abundant, then consumed when glucose falls and oxygen is available.

Ohm's-law pathway returns

\( f_G \equiv j_{E,C} \)

\( \Pi_F=\frac{Y_{ATP,F}}{1/f_G+r_F} \)

\( \Pi_R=\frac{Y_{ATP,R}}{1/f_G+r_{TCA}+r_{ETC}+1/f_{O_2}} \)

Dynamic splitter

\( U_F=\beta\Pi_F+\beta_a\ln\!\left(\frac{a_G}{a_C}\right),\qquad U_R=\beta\Pi_R \)

\( \theta_F^\ast=\frac{\exp(U_F)}{\exp(U_F)+\exp(U_R)} \)

\( \theta_R^\ast=\frac{\exp(U_R)}{\exp(U_F)+\exp(U_R)} \)

\( \frac{d\theta_F}{dt}=\frac{\theta_F^\ast-\theta_F}{\tau_{reg}} \)

\( j_F=\theta_Fj_{E,C},\qquad j_R^G=\theta_Rj_{E,C},\qquad \theta_R=1-\theta_F \)

\( j_R^{\mathrm{EtOH}}=j_{A,\mathrm{EtOH}},\qquad j_R=j_R^G+j_R^{\mathrm{EtOH}} \)

Growth and products

\( J_{ATP}=Y_{ATP,F}j_F+Y_{ATP,R}^Gj_R^G+Y_{ATP,\mathrm{EtOH}}j_R^{\mathrm{EtOH}} \)

\( \mu=Y_{V,F}j_F+Y_{V,R}^Gj_R^G+Y_{V,\mathrm{EtOH}}j_R^{\mathrm{EtOH}}-m_V \)

When catabolic supply is below \(m_V\), \(\mu<0\), representing mortality or living structural biomass loss from maintenance deficit.

Rationale For The Dynamic Splitter

\( U_F=\beta\Pi_F+\beta_a\ln\!\left(\frac{a_G}{a_C}\right),\qquad U_R=\beta\Pi_R \)

\( \theta_i^\ast=\frac{\exp(U_i)}{\exp(U_F)+\exp(U_R)},\qquad i\in\{F,R\} \)

\( \frac{d\theta_F}{dt}=\frac{\theta_F^\ast-\theta_F}{\tau_{reg}} \)

Bounded flux allocation

The softmax keeps each target allocation between 0 and 1 and enforces \(\theta_F^\ast+\theta_R^\ast=1\).

Pathway economics

\(U_F\) and \(U_R\) score fermentation and respiration using ATP return per effective pathway resistance; the larger score gets more carbon flux.

Glucose activity signal

\(\ln(a_G/a_C)\) is a dimensionless chemical-potential-like signal. For ideal dilute glucose, \(a_G/a_C \approx G/K_{Crabtree}\).

Ethanol as second substrate

\(j_{A,\mathrm{EtOH}}\) is oxygen dependent and multiplied by \(I_G(G)\), so ethanol uptake is repressed while glucose is abundant and rises after glucose depletion.

Finite regulation

\(\tau_{reg}\) represents delayed enzyme, proteome, redox, and mitochondrial adjustment; as \(\tau_{reg}\to 0\), the splitter becomes instantaneous.

Link to attention

Scaled dot-product attention uses \(\alpha_i=\exp(q\cdot k_i/\sqrt{d})/\sum_j\exp(q\cdot k_j/\sqrt{d})\). Here, pathway scores \(U_i\) play the role of attention scores, and the softmax returns carbon-allocation weights.

Carbon conservation

The glucose-derived branch fluxes satisfy \(j_F+j_R^G=j_{E,C}\). Ethanol respiration adds a separate substrate flux \(j_R^{\mathrm{EtOH}}\).

Low glucose, high O2

Glucose-derived respiration dominates if glucose remains low. Ethanol can also be consumed as a respiratory substrate.

High glucose, O2 present

Fermentation increases because the short pathway can deliver ATP faster per limiting pathway capacity.

Glucose depletion

The splitter relaxes back toward respiration, glucose repression weakens, and ethanol uptake can drive a diauxic respiratory phase if oxygen remains.

Why Make The Splitter Dynamic?

  • The algebraic splitter gives the Crabtree threshold.
  • The dynamic splitter adds finite regulatory time for proteome rewiring, mitochondrial capacity, redox handling, and enzyme expression.
  • Use the dynamic form for glucose pulses, diauxic shifts, hysteresis, and single-cell or microfluidic trajectories.

Symbol Key

Symbol Meaning Model role
\(G, G_{in}\) External glucose and its inflow concentration. Primary carbon substrate; drives reserve formation and the Crabtree glucose signal.
\(\mathrm{EtOH}, \mathrm{EtOH}_{in}\) External ethanol and its inflow concentration. Fermentation product that can become a secondary respiratory substrate after glucose depletion.
\(O_2, O_{2,in}, O_{2,sat}\) Dissolved oxygen, inflow oxygen, and air-equilibrium oxygen. Constrains the respiratory branch through oxygen delivery.
\(D, k_La\) Dilution rate and gas-transfer coefficient. Set chemostat exchange and oxygen reaeration; use \(D=0\) for batch culture.
\(j_{A,G}, j_{G,\max}, K_G\) Glucose assimilation rate, maximum glucose uptake capacity, and glucose half-saturation. Moves external glucose into internal carbon reserve.
\(j_{A,\mathrm{EtOH}}, j_{\mathrm{EtOH},\max}, K_{\mathrm{EtOH}}\) Ethanol uptake rate, maximum ethanol uptake capacity, and ethanol half-saturation. Adds ethanol as a second substrate feeding respiration.
\(I_G, K_{G,E}, n\) Glucose repression factor, repression scale, and Hill sharpness. Suppress ethanol uptake while glucose remains high.
\(x_C, j_{E,C}\) Carbon reserve density and mobilized reserve flux. Internal memory of glucose history and immediate catabolic supply.
\(v_{E,C}, K_{E,C}\) Maximum reserve-turnover rate and reserve-turnover half-saturation. Control how quickly stored carbon becomes usable flux.
\(V, \mu, m_V\) Living structural biomass, specific growth rate, and maintenance demand. Convert pathway fluxes into net biomass growth; negative \(\mu\) represents mortality or living structural loss.
\(f_G, f_{O_2}, k_O, K_O, K_{O,\mathrm{EtOH}}\) Glucose-derived catabolic supply, oxygen supply, oxygen-transfer coefficient, and oxygen half-saturations. Define glucose and oxygen conductances in the pathway comparison and oxygen limitation of ethanol uptake.
\(\Pi_F, \Pi_R\) ATP-generation return for fermentation and respiration. The splitter favors the branch with the larger return per pathway resistance.
\(Y_{ATP,F}, Y_{ATP,R}\) ATP yield of glucose fermentation and glucose-derived respiration. Respiration has higher yield, while fermentation can have lower pathway resistance.
\(Y_{ATP,\mathrm{EtOH}}\) ATP yield of ethanol respiration. Connects ethanol uptake to respiratory ATP supply.
\(r_F, r_{TCA}, r_{ETC}\) Fermentation, citric-acid-cycle, and electron-transport-chain resistances. Represent finite enzyme or pathway capacity.
\(\theta_F, \theta_R, \theta_F^\ast, \theta_R^\ast, \tau_{reg}\) Actual and target fermentation and respiration fractions, plus regulatory timescale. Let flux allocation lag behind changing glucose and oxygen conditions.
\(a_G, a_C, K_{Crabtree}\) Glucose activity, reference Crabtree activity, and its concentration approximation. Make the glucose bias dimensionless; for ideal dilute glucose, \(a_G/a_C \approx G/K_{Crabtree}\).
\(U_F, U_R, \beta, \beta_a\) Fermentation and respiration softmax scores, pathway-return sensitivity, and activity-signal sensitivity. Translate pathway returns and high-glucose activity into the target splitter.
\(j_F, j_R^G, j_R^{\mathrm{EtOH}}, j_R, q_{O_2}\) Fermentation flux, glucose-derived respiration, ethanol-derived respiration, total respiration, and oxygen demand per respiratory carbon flux. Partition glucose-derived reserve and add ethanol respiration to total oxygen demand.
\(J_{ATP}, Y_{V,F}, Y_{V,R}^G, Y_{V,\mathrm{EtOH}}\) Total ATP supply and biomass yields for fermentation, glucose respiration, and ethanol respiration. Connect catabolic pathway use to growth potential.
\(y_{\mathrm{EtOH}}, \lambda_{\mathrm{EtOH}}\) Ethanol yield from fermentation and ethanol loss rate. Track ethanol as both the measurable Crabtree product and a later substrate.
\(Y_{X,G}, Y_{X,\mathrm{EtOH}}\) Conversion yields from external glucose and ethanol to model carbon-flux units. Needed when substrate uptake and internal fluxes use different units.

Conceptual basis: modified dynamic energy budget reserve dynamics from Tang and Riley (2025), combined with the Ohm's-law fermentation-versus-respiration pathway comparison from Tang et al. (2021).
